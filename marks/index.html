The shared marks

Three marks that every figure draws the same way.

The figures on this page move;

This page is not part of the explainer. It shows the marks the figures share, so they can be checked once: the flow mark for anything in transit, the energy burst, and the ATP beads.

1. The flow mark

The style draws every line as the edge of a thing. A molecule riding the blood, or an electron hopping between proteins, needs a mark that means "this is on its way somewhere." It gets its own color job, flow: the subject's navy as an open line at 0.85, never a face. An open circle is an oxygen atom; a solid dot is a carbon atom or an electron. While it moves, a short 2 px tail trails on the side it came from, so even a still frame shows the direction.

2. The energy burst

When the end phosphate snaps off, six short rays spring out from the break and vanish in half a second. They are drawn in the look-here orange, so the burst counts as one flash: it is never shown at the same time as a flash or a keyword callout.

3. ATP

The full mark (Figure 1.1, Figure 2.1): adenine as a double ring and ribose as a five-sided ring, both context, and the three phosphate beads as the subject. The compact mark is for figures where ATP is a passenger (parts 3, 4, 8 and 9): the sugar and three small beads. ADP has two. A phosphate handed to a protein can be marked in orange while it is attached (Figure 4.1).
